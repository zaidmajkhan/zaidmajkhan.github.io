When small business automation pays for itself

Automating the wrong step wastes weeks. The right filter is boring and useful: frequency × cost of delay × error risk — then compare that to build/maintenance cost.

Score the work

  • Frequency — daily or weekly tasks beat rare one-offs
  • Cost of delay — lost leads and no-shows beat tidy spreadsheets
  • Error risk — copy-paste into CRM is fragile; one bad mapping hurts trust

High on all three → automate. High frequency but low delay cost → maybe a better checklist is enough.

Estimate payback without theater

Rough math that keeps you honest:

  • Hours/week spent on the task × loaded hourly cost
  • Plus estimated revenue leaked from slow response or no-shows
  • Minus build time and ongoing review

If payback is inside one to two months on realistic numbers, it’s worth a prototype. If the spreadsheet only works with optimistic conversion rates, sharpen the process instead.

Start smaller than you want

First workflows should be readable by the person who owns the inbox. Document triggers, templates, and exception paths. The goal isn’t a black box — it’s a system someone else can run on Monday.
